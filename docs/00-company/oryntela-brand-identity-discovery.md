## Brand foundation

### Personality

Premium, modern, credible, intelligent, simple, distinctive, enterprise-capable,
commercial and confident. The balancing trait is **calm**: Oryntela should look
capable because it makes complexity understandable, not because it makes the
screen louder.

Avoid generic AI, crypto, cybersecurity, gaming, cheap SaaS, robotic, juvenile,
overly futuristic and template-driven signals. Specifically avoid brains, circuits,
sparkles, robot heads, chat bubbles, rockets, generic upward arrows, bar charts,
targets, handshakes, globes, shields, hexagon-tech marks, infinity loops and
gradient blobs.

### Primary audiences

| Priority  | Audience                                             | What the identity must signal                                              |
| --------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1         | Business owner / CEO; Head of Sales / Sales Director | Commercial confidence, breadth without complexity, operational seriousness |
| 2         | Sales Manager                                        | Clear decisions, trustworthy evidence, coaching without surveillance       |
| 3         | Seller / AE / BDM                                    | Ease, momentum and a visible next step rather than administration          |
| 4         | Revenue or commercial operations                     | Coherence, consistency, data legibility and controlled integration         |
| Influence | IT/security stakeholder                              | Restraint, traceability and a product that does not look disposable        |

The first market is Australian relationship-led B2B sales. The identity should be
direct and unpretentious without Australian visual clichés, and broad enough to
work internationally.

### Visual objective

The first impression should be:

> **One clear, connected sales system. Intelligent enough to guide the next move;
> disciplined enough to trust.**

This puts clarity and commercial direction ahead of novelty. It leaves room for
Sales Brain and AI-assisted capability while keeping the brand useful if AI
terminology changes.

### Category recommendation

- **Primary internal category:** end-to-end sales platform.
- **Secondary phrase:** sales operating system.
- **Contextual descriptor:** AI-assisted sales intelligence and workflow.

“End-to-end sales platform” is clearest on first contact and reflects Prospect,
Engage, CRM, Pipeline, Forecast, Create, Deal Rooms and Handover. “Sales operating
system” is more distinctive but needs a sentence of explanation. “AI sales
platform” makes a technology the identity; “sales intelligence platform”
understates execution; “revenue platform” is too broad; and “CRM” misrepresents the
product.

### Internal positioning statement

> For Australian relationship-led B2B sales teams, Oryntela is an end-to-end sales
> platform that turns authorised customer context into clear, reviewable next action
> from prospecting through handover. Unlike disconnected point tools and
> administration-heavy CRMs, it connects Sales Brain, workflow, native records,
> pipeline, forecasting and customer-ready outputs in one evidence-aware system.

This is a working internal statement for future copy development. It is not final
public or legal copy.

### Brand voice

Copy should be clear, confident, commercial, specific, helpful and intelligent.
Lead with the user's outcome or decision, then show the evidence or constraint.
Prefer short sentences and concrete verbs. State unavailable, mock, review-only and
uncertain states honestly.

Prefer **“Know what to do next.”** to claims about revolutionising revenue with
cutting-edge AI. Avoid hype, vague transformation language, unexplained technical
terms, faux intimacy and dense corporate prose.

## Current product audit

### Product truth the identity must hold

The repository implements a broad sales-platform foundation rather than a single
CRM, chatbot or lead database. The brand must span:

- Home / Daily, Sales Brain, Search / Ask and reviewed Actions;
- Native CRM records, Accounts, People, Interactions, Evidence and Opportunity
  Workspace;
- Prospect, Target Markets, Engage, Campaigns and Events;
- Pipeline, Analytics / Insights, Targets, Forecast and Manager Intelligence;
- Create, approved-template presentations and deterministic Business Cases;
- Opportunity Deal Rooms and reviewed Closed-Won Handover;
- Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, HubSpot and Salesforce integration foundations;
  and
- commercial plans, test billing and Credits controls.

Provider-backed and commercial-live status still varies. The identity must not make
mock, inactive, gated or review-only paths look live. The
[Oryntela master product blueprint](../01-product/oryntela-master-product-blueprint.md)
remains the status source.

### Current visual system

The implemented product is light-only and uses Arial/Helvetica, a warm off-white
page background (`#f5f7f4`), white surfaces, slate text and borders, teal primary
actions/focus states, and amber/rose/emerald/sky/indigo supporting states. The shell
uses a teal rounded-square “R” placeholder beside “RevenueOS”. Cards commonly use
12–24px corner radii, fine borders and restrained shadows.

The audit reviewed source tokens and current synthetic-data screenshots for Home,
Pipeline, Insights/Forecast, Prospect, Create, integrations, billing and Credits.
There is no committed favicon, app icon, logo asset, OpenGraph image, custom font or
dark-mode implementation. Root metadata still says “RevenueOS — Sales Brain”.

#### What works and should be retained structurally

- Calm light surfaces, generous spacing and a consistent content width.
- Clear page headings, plain descriptions and goal-based navigation.
- Visible focus treatment, 44px-class controls and reduced-motion support.
- Honest development/test/provider labels and restrained elevation.
- Semantic amber, rose/red, emerald and sky/indigo states that are not treated as
  decorative brand colours.
- Product screenshots and data views that can carry the marketing story without
  generic illustration.
- Mobile emphasis on Today, Interactions, Actions and Search rather than desktop
  parity.

#### Rebrand opportunities

- Arial is legible but provides almost no proprietary character.
- Repeated Tailwind teal/slate combinations look competent but category-generic.
- Brand, semantic and chart colours are used directly rather than through a small,
  explicit token layer; WO-052 should separate those roles.
- The placeholder “R” tile cannot carry recognition at favicon or social size.
- Very rounded cards and repeated uppercase micro-eyebrows can feel template-led
  when overused.
- “RevenueOS” is embedded in user-visible guidance, API errors, seeded pipeline
  names, generated PowerPoint properties, exports and tests—not only the shell.
- Public Deal Room, generated material, auth, empty/error and commercial surfaces
  need a coordinated rollout, not a shell-only rename.

### Differentiation opportunity

The category clusters around blue trust, purple AI gradients, green workflow,
sparkles/starbursts and product-panel montages. Oryntela can own a more disciplined

## Four visual directions

These are concept briefs only. The descriptions intentionally stop short of a final
logo drawing.

### Direction 1 — Meridian

- **Brand idea:** Commercial direction without a compass cliché. Oryntela helps a
  team orient around the evidence and make the next deliberate move.
- **Logo concept:** A compact symbol built from two or three unequal paths resolving
  around a precise negative-space point. The silhouette should imply orientation
  and convergence without drawing an arrow, compass needle or letter `O`.
- **Logo architecture:** Symbol + wordmark.
- **Wordmark:** `Oryntela` in controlled title case. A subtle angled terminal on the
  `Y` or `A` may echo the mark; no multiple custom tricks.
- **Colour:** Meridian Ink — midnight ink, deep mineral blue, restrained copper
  signal and warm white.
- **Typography:** Geist Sans or a similarly economical neo-grotesk; tabular figures
  for data.
- **UI feel:** Premium/minimal. Strong hierarchy, fine borders, slightly tighter
  radii and rare copper moments against calm light surfaces.
- **Why it fits:** Direction and precision connect naturally to a sales operating
  system, while the mark can remain abstract and enterprise-ready.
- **Risks / clichés:** A path can become a location-pin, paper-plane or generic
  upward arrow. Reject any version that needs motion or a gradient to read.

### Direction 2 — Convergence

- **Brand idea:** Many parts of the sales process becoming one coherent operating
  system.
- **Logo concept:** An abstract geometric mark made from three distinct modules
  locking into one stable form. Offset joins can create a subtle negative-space `N`
  without turning the result into a literal monogram or infinity loop.
- **Logo architecture:** Abstract mark + wordmark.
- **Wordmark:** `ORYNTELA` may be explored in spaced capitals for display, with
  `Oryntela` retained in product prose. Geometry should be echoed in only one
  letter, likely `N` or `T`.
- **Colour:** Spruce Signal — deep spruce, mineral teal, high-visibility citron used
  sparingly and soft grey-green neutrals.
- **Typography:** IBM Plex Sans with IBM Plex Mono reserved for technical metadata
  and dense figures.
- **UI feel:** Intelligent/technical with commercial energy. Modular layouts,
  explicit joins and crisp selected states; no circuit motifs.
- **Why it fits:** It expresses platform breadth and orchestration without tying the
  company to CRM, email, analytics or one module.
- **Risks / clichés:** Interlocking forms can resemble blockchain, cybersecurity,
  recycling or an infinity symbol. The silhouette must remain asymmetric,
  ownable-looking and legible at 16px.

### Direction 3 — Oryntela Standard

- **Brand idea:** Confidence through naming and restraint. The invented word is the
  recognisable asset; the system gets out of the user's way.
- **Logo concept:** A custom or carefully modified wordmark first. A compact symbol
  is derived later from one proven letter feature—potentially the `Y` junction or
  `O` counter—only if it remains recognisable independently.
- **Logo architecture:** Wordmark-led with minimal symbol.
- **Wordmark:** Test `Oryntela` and `ORYNTELA`; do not default to lowercase. Explore
  one subtle proprietary move, such as a directional `Y` junction or a cut shared
  by `N` and `A`.
- **Colour:** Editorial Cobalt — disciplined cobalt, softer periwinkle, coral signal
  and warm paper neutrals. The wordmark must also work in black.
- **Typography:** Geist Sans for UI and a bespoke wordmark drawing; alternatively
  Source Sans 3 if a more human rhythm is preferred.
- **UI feel:** The quietest and easiest route. Typography, space and real product UI
  carry the identity; accents are rare.
- **Why it fits:** “Oryntela” is distinctive enough to lead. This route minimises
  symbol cliché, performs well in navigation and supports a premium, enduring
  software identity.
- **Risks / clichés:** An unmodified open-source font will feel unfinished; excessive
  letter spacing can feel like consulting or luxury branding; a derived favicon
  must not be a generic `O` tile.

### Direction 4 — Copper Field

- **Brand idea:** Commercial judgement is a human discipline, strengthened by
  intelligence. Oryntela feels established, useful and assured rather than cold or
  futuristic.
- **Logo concept:** A compact abstract letterform built from a stable field and one
  deliberate opening. The opening can hint at clarity emerging from complexity or
  a subtle `O/T` relationship, but no literal explanation is required.
- **Logo architecture:** Abstract letterform/monogram + wordmark.
- **Wordmark:** `Oryntela` with slightly editorial proportions and a confident,
  conventional baseline. Consider a refined serif only for the wordmark or
  marketing display—not product UI.
- **Colour:** Aubergine & Copper — deep aubergine, muted plum, earthy copper and warm
  parchment.
- **Typography:** Source Sans 3 for UI with Source Serif 4 used selectively for
  marketing headlines and generated-material covers.
- **UI feel:** Warm/approachable and premium. Matte colour, documentary product
  screenshots, restrained rules and little decorative animation.
- **Why it fits:** It is unexpected in sales software without becoming playful. It
  supports trust, Australian directness and customer-facing documents especially
  well.
- **Risks / clichés:** Warm neutrals can drift into accounting, legal or lifestyle;
  a serif can reduce UI clarity; copper must not become a metallic raster effect.

### Colour-system rules

- The final core mark must work in one colour; gradients are optional support only.
- Brand primary, secondary and accent must be separate from success, warning, error
  and information.
- Do not turn semantic red or green into decoration that changes its meaning.
- Reserve at least five distinguishable chart series outside the primary brand
  accent, with patterns/labels where colour alone is insufficient.
- Focus must remain unmistakable on white, tinted and dark surfaces.
- Module accents, if used, should be subtle tints or one shared secondary family.
  Do not create rainbow module branding.

## Logo-system and technical requirements

The later approved identity must provide:

- primary logo and wordmark;
- standalone symbol/mark;
- horizontal and useful compact lockups;
- monochrome, light-background and dark-background versions;
- favicon, app-style square icon and useful social/profile icon;
- clean SVG masters and only required PNG derivatives; and
- practical clear-space, minimum-size, background and incorrect-use rules.

The design acceptance matrix is:

| Context                        | Requirement                                                                                                                  |
| ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 16px favicon                   | Recognisable silhouette; no type, hairlines or dependent colour detail                                                       |
| 32px favicon                   | Clear internal counter/negative space without blur or clipping                                                               |
| 48px icon                      | Works in a square without an arbitrary enclosing tile                                                                        |
| Product navigation             | Wordmark/lockup remains calm beside dense UI and at common zoom levels                                                       |
| Login and marketing header     | Strong on light and dark backgrounds                                                                                         |
| Documents, print and grayscale | One-colour form remains identifiable and professional                                                                        |
| SVG                            | Valid view box, no clipping, no embedded raster, no unnecessary masks/filters and accessible title where rendered as content |

Do not approve a concept that only works with a gradient, glow, fine line or
animation. Device/logo clearance remains professionally unverified even after a
visual direction is selected.

### Methodology

The round deliberately holds context and craft constant while varying logo
architecture. M1–M3 use one Meridian palette and S1–S2 use one Standard palette so
colour does not decide the route by accident. The marks were constructed from
simple vector geometry or typography, then tested without gradient in colour,
black on white, white on black, at 16/32/48px, in a neutral app icon, and in
synthetic header, login and future marketing-header contexts. No customer data,
stock logo generator, paid font, remote runtime dependency or production UI was
used.

Geist Sans is the concept-round type reference. Its
[official repository](https://github.com/vercel/geist-font) states that the family
is licensed under the SIL Open Font License 1.1. No font file is distributed in
this round; the concept SVGs retain local system fallbacks. Final wordmark
outlines, optical kerning and the required licence file belong to the final WO-051
identity package only after owner selection.
