The proposed commercial use is a business-to-business online software product for
relationship-driven sales teams. It is intended to bring deliberately authorised
interaction evidence, account and contact context, opportunities, actions,
methodology and explainable commercial analysis into one reviewable operating
surface. It complements external systems of record and can also support a bounded
native CRM workflow. It is not positioned as a generic CRM or an autonomous bulk
outreach system.

### Product-use boundary

This product summary is based on the repository's master product blueprint,
executive product map, commercial handoff and first-design-partner launch gate. It
is included to help an adviser draft an accurate specification; it does not
authorise a launch or assert that every built component is commercially in use.

#### BUILT

- Core tenant-isolated company, contact, opportunity, task, meeting, interaction
  evidence and AI job/artefact foundations.
- Core sales workflows covering Home/Daily, Accounts and People, Opportunities and
  Pipeline, Actions, Search/Ask, methodology, analytics, targets, transparent
  forecasting and manager intelligence.
- Bounded Native CRM, presentation/business-case creation, Prospect, Engage and
  Event foundations, with focused HubSpot architecture.
- Evidence-aware, review-first and explainable product boundaries rather than
  unsupervised decision-making.

#### PARTIALLY BUILT

- Production provider-backed interaction capture and AI intelligence are not
  generally active; several normal paths remain deterministic, mock-backed,
  simulated or gated.
- Prospect, Engage, provider execution and focused CRM sync have implemented
  foundations but do not support an unrestricted public service claim.
- Private-beta operations remain subject to the named launch gates, approvals and
  proof requirements.

#### FUTURE / VALIDATE FIRST

- A fuller company/selling profile and a stronger `What changed?` surface.
- SMS, native mobile/background capture, broader files support and voice or SDR
  capabilities, subject to validation, authority, privacy and provider gates.
- Any future international market, downloadable app or separately sold training or
  consultancy service. No international expansion plan has been approved.

#### INTENTIONALLY NOT BUILDING

- Salesforce parity, a generic project manager, general document repository,
  generic workflow platform or generic BI suite.
- Employee surveillance/ranking, private buyer dossiers, manipulative persuasion
  or opaque scores and forecasts.
- A generic autonomous SDR or bulk-spam engine.

The intended commercial outcome is to preserve the option to launch and invest in
the Oryntela brand for the actual product above, with proportionate protection and
without claiming unused goods or services. Whether that outcome is achievable
under this mark **requires qualified trade-mark advice**.

## 5. Filing-order and jurisdiction questions

The current strategy supports Australia as the first market. It does not support
spending in speculative jurisdictions. WIPO states that a Madrid international
application relies on a national or regional basic mark, and IP Australia's
[international-application guidance](https://manuals.ipaustralia.gov.au/trademark/2.2-international-application-form)
states that an Australian international application must be based on a mark
applied for or registered with IP Australia. WIPO and IP Australia materials refer
to a six-month convention-priority period. Whether and how those rules apply here
**requires qualified trade-mark advice**.

Questions for sequencing advice:

1. Does Australia-first match the actual initial market and evidence of intended
   use, or is a different first filing justified? **Requires qualified trade-mark
   advice.**
2. If an Australian application is filed, what date would start the convention
   priority period, and what decisions must be made within six months? **Requires
   qualified trade-mark advice.**
3. Would later Madrid filing be appropriate only after specific commercial
   countries are approved, or would direct national filings ever be preferable?
   **Requires qualified trade-mark advice.**
4. How do ORYNTECH in the EU and ORYNTE in Türkiye affect any filing sequence,
   designation decision or priority analysis? **Requires qualified trade-mark
   advice.**
5. Should the plain word mark be the first asset considered? IP Australia notes
   that plain word/phrase marks and logos are different forms and that businesses
