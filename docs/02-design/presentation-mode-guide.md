# Presentation Mode implementation guide

## Current experience

Presentation Mode is a browser-first specialisation of an Interaction, not a separate presentation product. A presentation Interaction uses the existing preparation brief, visual evidence and post-interaction debrief surfaces.

Before the presentation, preparation prioritises objectives, audience and stakeholder roles, likely objections, proof points, customer questions and a clear closing step. During and after the presentation, the visual surface offers quick paths for a customer-question photo, whiteboard or presentation context.

## Evidence boundary

Seller-prepared slides and deck pages are labelled `salesperson_created`. They may be retained as context but can never become customer-confirmed buying signals, decisions, budget, timeline, procurement or commitment evidence. The UI repeats this boundary at capture, saved-visual and review points.

Customer-created and jointly created sources may produce reviewable candidates. Unknown-origin material keeps that uncertainty. AI interpretation and source ownership remain separate concepts.

## Presentation debrief

The bounded debrief prioritises:

1. audience reactions and discussion;
2. customer questions;
3. objections or hesitation;
4. requested material or follow-up;
5. decision-path or stakeholder changes; and
6. commitments and the next meeting.

Extraction additionally filters seller-material statements such as “our deck says” from signal categories. Accepted debrief items remain labelled “Reported by you”.

## Mobile and accessibility

The web surface uses large labelled controls, a rear-camera browser hint, a keyboard-operable drop zone, visible focus, semantic progress and explicit error/status messages. The selected image remains local until the user confirms upload. There is no background capture, screen-lock recording or offline sync claim.

## Out of scope

Slide authoring, deck editing, PowerPoint/Keynote integration, projector control, live audience analysis, real-time transcription, recording and native mobile capture remain out of scope.
